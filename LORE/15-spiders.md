**Spiders**

This article is about the Guardian device. For other uses, see Spider (*disambiguation*). Spiders (*SPIDERSAUGH*) are devices (*constructs*) placed on locations around the interwebs to protect travelers and define boundaries.

**Etymology**

Spiders were purposefully designed to resemble their arachnid counterparts. The non-spherical body supported on eight hydraulically powered legs is intentionally reminiscent of the "eight-legged freaks" of yore. The official term for spiders is Automated Deterant Device.

**Development and Use**

After the Fall, the interwebs became a dangerous place for travelers. Even though means of traversing were scarce and the distances traveled were minimal, the interwebs seemed to be forever strewn with debris of the fallen Civilization. Much of that debris was prone to explosive combustion. This precipitated the formation of the Guardians class, beginning strictly as a policing and enforcement force and then, as civilization was rebuilt, as one of the three official institutions burdened with the regulation of interwebs policies.

Although the requirement for protection was indeed covered by shields and their different incarnations (*cf. shields*), the need for an incisive thrust against the various dangers of the interwebs, both modern and ancient, soon became evident to Council of the Guardians. For the purposes of researching, developing, and implementing such a device, the Council turned to Yuki Michaux a well-noted member of the Guardians. Ms. Michaux experimented with various designs of devices before arriving at the spider design (her prototype of the Venomous Caterpillar has recently been uncovered in the ruins of the Tawny Portal workrooms).

Present-day spiders operate with a combination of olfactory and movement detectors that react to the proximity of explosives, seeking them out and causing premature detonation of the explosives (which in effect renders them harmless while causing damage to the carrier). Current Union Alliance policy is that placement of spiders in domains across the interwebs is obligatory for the common good and follows the "Safe Controlled and Order" directive of AF 235
